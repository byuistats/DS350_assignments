Team Meeting Instructions

Overview

Students have found these meetings to be an important and engaging way to improve their coding skills, visualization skills, code review skills, data intuition and data validation skills.

Each task and case study has many possible solutions. There is always more than one way to complete the assignment. No two students will do things exactly the same.

Communicating what decisions and steps went into your analysis is a critical skill in the workplace. Learning to evaluate validity of others conclusions and analysis is a common task in the workplace. Collaborating and melding ideas can add a lot of value.

Students should share what they have learned and ask questions. Don’t let this meeting simply be another version of the Data Science lab or a tutoring sessions. This can be avoided by not limiting your discussion to only syntax and code. The most invigorating and useful discussions often focus on the overall conclusions and answers to the assignment questions, without worrying about code. These discussions often give birth to new considerations that ultimately improve your approach or visualization.

Objective

These group meetings will be an opportunity for students to learn from each other as they share what they have learned, ask questions, and gain ideas primarily through review of peers’ code and output.

Students will become better critical thinkers, effective communicators, and skilled collaborators through discussing their work and making/challenging data based arguments to answer a question.

A side benefit of these meetings is to remove a feeling of isolation. When involved in challenging coding assignments, joining with others can boost morale, creativity, and hope as you see your peers also struggle and ultimately overcome. It will provide an avenue to build your network.

Preparation for Group Meetings

To be considered “prepared” for the group meetings, students should have their work for the 3 most recent coding assignments pushed to GitHub and ready to share. Placing a link to the .md file in the group’s slack channel before the meeting is highly recommended. Each student should have attempted/considered the entire assignment, but may not have answers to every single obstacle they encountered or have a completely polished product.

Group Meeting Agenda

  1. If needed, make introductions.
  2. Verify who has something pushed to GitHub and take time to open those repos/click on the links.
  3. Address any issues team members have with accessing/reading in the data, loading necessary packages, or understanding instructions.
  4. Discuss key assumptions/definitions that needed to be made or required for data cleaning. Share how were these implemented or addressed.
  5. Look at the visualization. Specifically, discuss the following:
    • If applicable: What is the final recommendation/answer to the question?
      • If group members are getting different results, is it because of the visualization or the underlying data?
      • What evidence in the chart specifically lead to that answer/recommendation?
    • Did the visualization satisfy all the requirements of the assignment? If not, what needs to be added/changed?
    • What remaining questions/counter arguments still exist?
    • What other visualizations could have been tried? What other geoms could have been explored?
  6. How could the visualization be strengthened/improved?
  7. Walk through the highlights of your code. Share things you were proud to have discovered or places where you got stuck.

Accountability

Once a week, students will fill out a survey in I-learn that asks them to rate themselves and their teammates on these 3 questions:

  • Was the person prepared (see Preparation for Group Meetings)?

  • Did the person participate and engage during the meeting?

  • Did the person arrive on time and stay for the entirety of the meeting?
